## I. INTRODUCTION

Dekisugi Tincle Pack is a Nintendo DSiWare title consisting of two applications and three mini-games: (1) Tarot fortune-telling, (2) Timer, (3) Calculator with check splitter, (4) Dancing game, and (5) Coin flipping game.

It was released to promote the then-upcoming game Ripened Tingle's Balloon Trip of Love (released August 6, 2009).

Dekisugi Tincle Pack was released on the Nintendo DSi Shop in Japan on June 24, 2009. It could be purchased for 500 DS points until it was removed on January 6, 2010.

The game contains Japanese text only. This guide provides Japanese-to-English translation of menu elements and prompts, and it includes a complete walkthrough. This file is encoded in UTF-8. It should be read with a monospace font.

## IV. Walkthrough

### Title Screen

The title screen features a suitcase showcasing the game's components. 

Press A to view the game's instruction manual. Press X to close the suitcase and send it into space. The game's credits are displayed.

Tap once on an application in the suitcase to view its name. Tap a second time to start the application. These are the applications:
* Tarot fortune-telling
* Timer
* Calculator with check splitter
* Dancing game
* Coin-flipping game

---

### Tarot - Fortune Telling

In the lower-left corner is a book. Tap it to view previously revealed cards. Tap a card to view its text. Tap "Return" to return to fortune-telling.

Tap the bell in the center of the table to summon the fortune teller. The fortune teller appears and asks you to select which type of fortune you would like read: (1) financial; (2) romantic; or (3) "Kooloo Limpah". She may refuse to appear if you have requested too many fortunes in a short time. In that case wait a few hours. Select one of the fortune types, and confirm that it is the one you want. 

Tap the deck of cards to spread the cards on the table. Tap anywhere in the pile of cards and move the stylus around the screen to shuffle the cards. Tap "finished" when you are done shuffling. The cards are assembled into a tidy stack. Tap an arrow to rotate the deck clockwise or counter-clockwise. In Tarot, the orientation of a card is important to the interpretation of the card. 

The fortune teller lays out the cards in an array. Tap a card twice to select it. The fortune teller reveals the card and renders an interpretation. The Google Translate app does a fair job of translating the Japanese text here, since it is black text on a light background. 

Tap the screen to end the reading, and then confirm that you would like to end the fortune-telling session. The fortune teller states that you are welcome to come again. If you discovered a previously unseen card, the word "New" (in English) appears on the book.

If you ring the bell and the fortune teller refuses to appear, you can keep ringing the bell. Eventually, she will demand that you go home. 

Tap "Return" when you are ready to return to the title screen. Confirm your choice to leave the fortune-telling game.

---

### Timer

Tap the up or down triangle on the left pair of buttons to set the minutes. Tap the up or down triangle on the right pair of buttons to set the seconds. 

Tap the start/stop button to start the timer. Press it again to pause the timer. Pause the timer and tap the reset button to reset the timer. 

When the timer counts down to 00:00, the Tingle head gyrates and makes bizarre vocalizations. Tap the start/stop button to stop the alarm.

Tap "Return" when you are ready to return to the title screen. Confirm your choice. 

---

### Calculator

The calculator application includes a standard calculator and a secretarial calculator. The application has two tabs. The left is the standard calculator, and the right is the secretarial calculator.

#### Standard Calculator

Tap the buttons on the calculator to perform arithmetic operations. 

#### Secretarial Calculator

The secretarial calculator helps split a restaurant check when some participants belong to a group who will pay more per person and other participants belong to a group who will pay less per person. This might occur when managers take a team to a restaurant and buy appetizers and drinks for everyone, but the meal is split evenly. 

Enter the money amount of the bill. The app indicates that the value is in Yen, but the unit of currency is unimportant. Tap the large accept button below the "C" to accept the amount. 

Use the left and right arrows next to the blue box to indicate the number of people who will pay more per person. Use the left and right arrows next to the red box to indicate the number of people who will pay less per person. Tap the accept button. 

Tap and drag the slider to adjust the fraction to be paid by each group. The numbers in the top screen will update based on the selection. Select a denomination: 1, 10, 100, or 1000. This indicates that the split will be performed in these units. The top screen displays the overpayment that results from selecting the denomination.  

Example: 
* 10050 Yen bill.
* Two people in the blue group.
* Ten people in the red group.
* Slider is halfway between the center and the blue side. 
* Denomination is set to "1".

This combination allocates 2775 Yen of cost to each person in the blue group and 450 Yen of cost to each person in the red group. Suppose the 100 Yen denomination is selected. The contribution of each person in the blue group is increased to 3100 Yen, and the contribution of each person in the red group is reduced to 400 Yen. The total payment is (3100 * 2) + (10 * 400) = 10200, and the upper screen displays the overpayment of 150 Yen. Perhaps the overage is meant to go toward the tip.

Tap "Return" when you are ready to return to the title screen. Confirm your choice. 

---

### Dancer

Tingle stands in the upper screen as if he were a marionette in a diorama. Tap one of the four buttons on the left side of the screen to change Tingle's outfit, background, and music. These are the options:
* Green: Meadow theme.
* Blue: Tropical theme.
* Yellow: Disco theme.
* White: Ballet theme.

Click and drag the crank in a circle to make Tingle dance. The speed of rotation determines the tempo of the music and the speed of Tingle's dance. The vertical bar on the right side of the screen indicates the tempo. The ideal tempo is marked by the green line in the center of the gauge. 

While Tingle is dancing, pictures from the console's camera roll are displayed in the background. If photos cannot be displayed, an error message is displayed. 

There is no scoring; it appears that the goal is to stay at your preferred tempo. The dance stops prematurely if you stray outside of the green track on the crank's wheel. 

Tap "Return" when you are ready to return to the title screen. Confirm your choice.

---

### Coin-Flipping

There are three modes, but the first two are identical except for their graphics. Both feature flipping coins featuring either a version of Tingle's face or a failure message. The third mode includes only numbers.

#### Face Modes

Tap the first or second mode.

The object of the game is to find all the coins featuring Tingle's face before finding all of the coins with a failure message. Use the top set of left and right arrows to select the total number of coins in the game. Use the second set of arrows to select the number of coins featuring Tingle's face. The remaining coins feature a failure message. 

Example: 
* Four total coins.
* Two coins with Tingle's face.
* Two coins with a failure message. 

Tap the start button. 

Tap a coin to select it, and tap a second time to confirm the selection. Your selection is recorded on the score board on the top screen. In this example, you win if you find both Tingle coins before you find both failure coins. You can find one failure coin and still win. 

#### Counting Mode

Tap the third option, which is a counting mode.

The object of the game is to reveal all coins in numerical order. Use the left and right arrows to select the number of coins. Tap the start button to begin. 

Example: 
* Two total coins, numbered "1" and "2".

Tap a coin to select it, and tap a second time to confirm the selection. If the coin is not the next in sequence, you lose. If it is the next in sequence, you continue playing. If you complete the sequence, you win. 

Tap "Return" when you are ready to return to the title screen. Confirm your choice.

## V. Text Acquisition

This section is useful for players who want to translate the game's entire text, not just its menu and prompt text. 

1. Legally obtain a copy of the ROM of this game.

The game is a DSiWare title and was on the Nintendo eShop for a limited time and only in Japan. Legally obtaining the ROM probably means acquiring a Nintendo DSi or 3DS that has the game installed and then using one of many tools to dump it. ROM dumping will not be discussed here.


2. Open with CrystalTile2

http://www.romhacking.net/utilities/818/
In CrystalTile2, select Tools -> NDS File System. This displays the files stored in the ROM's file system. Text is contained in files named like FSI.CT/overlay9_00**.bin. Notice the "LZ" icon next to each file. This means that each is compressed with an LZ algorithm, probably LZ10.


3. Extract Files

Select the overlay9** files and decompress them. Select File -> Extract(U), and then select a directory on your file system. CrystalTile2 writes decompressed files to the specified directory. 


4. Read Files as Shift-JIS

The text of this game is in Japanese, typically the Hiragana and Katakana scripts, but sometimes the Han script. They are encoded using the "shift-JIS" encoding. In each file, the text is interspersed with binary. It is possible to view the text fairly well using Notepad++.

To work with the text, I recommend reading it programmatically, using Python 3. The following lines do a pretty good job of reading the text of a single file. 

```python
path = "overlay9_0015.bin"
with open(path, "r", encoding='shift-jis', errors="ignore") as f:
    text = f.read()
```

Use a `for` loop to replace "0015" with values "0000" through "0029" to read the files sequentially. 


5. Find Usable Text

The text files are probably of a known format and store offsets to each block of text in a binary structure. 

I was satisfied to extract most of the text quickly. The following regular expression and Python 3 code does a fair job of finding usable text. The code prints usable text to the screen.

```python
import re

regex = r'\w{5,}'
chunks = re.findall(regex, text)
for chunk in chunks:
    print(chunk)
```


6. Submit Text to Google Translate or Bing.

For large segments of text, consider using an API to Google Translate. Sometimes Google Translate produces meaningless results. Bing may perform better.


7. Manual Handling

Not all text is stored in .BIN files; some may be stored as tile graphics. There are tools that can help identify characters manually.

a. BabelMap: Most Japanese characters used in this game are found in Unicode range U+3040 - U+30FF. BabelMap provides a convenient way to view all characters quickly. Similar characters often appear next to each other.  
b. Google Translate handwriting entry. It is possible to draw a Japanese character using the mouse with Google Translate. It does a great job of displaying matches that are similar to the handwritten character.
